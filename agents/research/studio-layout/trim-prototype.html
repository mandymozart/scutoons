How it works

A · Mobile

iPhone Photos, CapCut, Instagram

How it behaves

  • The take is one filmstrip; a frame with two thick grips lies over it, the rest is dimmed.
  • Drag a grip: the big picture shows the frame under the grip, with its time.
  • Hold a grip still for half a second: the strip zooms to single frames under it; let go and it zooms back. Pinch or Ctrl + wheel zooms too.
  • Tap the picture (or Space) to play the kept part once.

Try

Drag the left grip, then hold it still. Tap the picture.

Good for the studio

  • One thumb, nothing to learn; the frame you cut on is the big picture.
  • The natural trim for the phone page (Handheld) – the timeline is hidden on phones.

Not so good

  • A mode of its own: one take at a time, no neighbours, no time axis, Cancel / Done.
  • At full width a frame is a sliver; precision only by holding or zooming.
  • A mouse and keys get no more than a finger.

B · Ableton

Live's arrangement + clip view

How it behaves

  • In the arrangement a clip is a window onto its take. Drag its left or right edge (the cursor turns into a bracket): the picture stays where it is in time, only the window opens or closes.
  • Drag the clip to move it; Alt-drag slips the take inside the window – the window stays, the picture moves.
  • Edges snap to the grid, and the grid follows the zoom (seconds, parts of a second, frames). Hold Alt while dragging for single frames.
  • The clip view shows the whole take with a start and an end marker – the same cut; outside them is greyed. ← → nudge a marker by a frame, Shift ×10.

Try

Drag the left edge of s12, Alt-drag its picture, Ctrl + wheel over the arrangement and drag again.

Good for the studio

  • Precise: a grid, a modifier, keys.
  • Slip is a real tool: keep the length, choose the moment.

Not so good

  • The cut parts vanish from the arrangement – you only see what you cut away in the clip view (against "order is space").
  • A trimmed clip leaves a gap; nothing closes up.
  • Two places for one cut, modifiers a phone doesn't have, a lot of UI.

C · Studio – recommended

the built timeline, grown by the best of A and B

How it behaves

  • Take mode shows the whole take; the cut parts stay in place, hatched (as built) – you see what you cut away.
  • Grab the in or out line anywhere along its height (Ableton's edge, the bracket cursor) or by its tab (finger-sized on a touch screen).
  • While you trim, the main view shows the in and the out frame side by side; the one you move is marked.
  • Edges snap to frames. The bar under the kept part – or Alt-drag on its picture – slips the kept part through the take: same length, another moment.
  • Hold a grip still: the timeline zooms to single frames under it; let go and it zooms back. Ctrl + wheel, + / − / 0 as now.
  • ← → a frame, Shift ×10 on a focused grip, the slip bar or the ruler (the playhead); I / O = in / out at the playhead. Play plays the kept parts in order (Space here – the studio itself has no Space key).

Try

Drag the out line of s12, hold it still, slip with the bar, put the playhead somewhere and press I.

Built today

The hatched cut parts, the in / out lines grabbable along their height, frame steps, ← → (Shift ×10), the playhead + 3D view at the moved point, Play skipping the cut parts, Ctrl + wheel, + / − / 0.

New

The bracket cursors + the tab, the in / out pair in the main view, slip, I / O, hold to zoom, the readouts.

Good for the studio

  • One place, spatial, mouse and finger, keys for precision.
  • No new data: the take keeps its in / out.

Not so good

  • The lane carries pictures, grips and the slip bar – busier at zoom 1.
  • The pair in the main view needs a second picture of the 3D view (one still when a drag starts).
