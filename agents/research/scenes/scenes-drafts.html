1 · What you saw: four strips of the same shape read from the code, today

The chapter tree, the storyboard, the show's rows band and OSCT's scenes panel all show an ordered row of units, and some neighbours are bundled together. Under four different names they are the same thing. A deck scene is not on this list: it is what a unit plays on a stage.

2 · The model proposal

A sequence is an ordered list of steps. Neighbouring steps that are shown and loaded together form a window. A step has one lane per output, which says what plays there, plus how the step is entered (fire) and how it ends (end). The players (reader, deck, XR viewer) each draw only their own lane.

page lane: slices (reader)stage lane: a deck scene / rig / module + values (deck) target lane: an XR scene (XR viewer)light lane: a look (DMX)

A chapter

sequence = the chapter · window = a chain (continues) · step = a group · lanes: page (+ stage if a group names a deck scene, S5) · end = group.end / the next chapter

A show

sequence = the show · window = one row · step = a row · lanes: one stage per outlet, light per device · fire = Row.fire (event, auto / suggest, moment)

An XR experience

sequence = the experience · window = an OSCT group · step = an XR scene · lane: target · fire = its image target is found (random access)

3 · Drivers move one cursor who decides when to flip

The cursor is where the sequence stands now. Whatever moves it is a driver: the reader's scroll, a story event, the conductor, the XR tracker, a timer. "Live" and "reading" are only two different drivers on the same sequence. A driver's own policy (suggest vs autopilot, hold time, bar or phrase) stays with that driver.

4 · Flows five situations, one model

Each flow shows who moves the cursor (left) and who draws which lane (right). Grey arrows exist today; blue arrows are new in this concept.

F1 · Reading alone

The same as today. Only next() and the lookahead come from the core (S4); the reader looks the same.

F2 · A linked reader drives decks, without a show

New (S5): a group names a deck scene in its stage lane. The reader scrolls into the group, and the session's decks crossfade to that scene. A show row can still override it.

F3 · A conductor runs a show

The same as today, with every feature kept. GO-next and what to preload come from the core (S3). A deck playing a show alone holds a window of rows, not all of them.

F4 · XR: a found target

Random access: the camera decides. The order only says what to preload next (OSCT's ±2 window, from the core, S8).

F5 · Chaining across kinds: one end, any step address

S9: end and jumps take any step address, e.g. chapter:3.harbour → book:vinyl.cover → back → a show row's event. The reader already jumps to xr, but no XR route exists yet.

5 · Loading: one window rule, each player's own budget prepare · keep · release

window(sequence, cursor, budget) returns what to prepare, nearest first and cancellable, what to keep, and what to release. The budget belongs to the player: the reader's data tier, GPU memory, the deck's quality governor.
